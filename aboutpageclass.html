Project AxelNode

AxelNode is a custom-engineered smart mobility and sensor platform built on a wagon cart chassis. Powered by a Raspberry Pi 5, it integrates advanced computer vision, long-distance proximity sensing, and robust motor control to create an interactive, semi-autonomous hardware node.

The core mission of AxelNode was to build a mobile, intelligent unit capable of processing its environment locally via various sensors while providing a user-friendly touch interface right on the hardware. All code and scripts powering this system are fully open-source.

Hardware BOM & Architecture

A comprehensive breakdown of the physical components used to build AxelNode, detailing exactly what they do and why they are critical to the project's success.

Raspberry PI 5 ✅

Role: The central "brain" of the AxelNode.
Importance: It runs the main operating system, processes all sensor data, handles the camera feed, and executes the code that dictates the cart's behavior. The Pi 5's upgraded processing power is essential for smooth realtime operations.

Arducam IR

Role: Infrared camera module.
Importance: Provides machine vision capabilities to the node, allowing it to "see" its environment even in low-light or zero-light conditions by detecting infrared light, which is crucial for nighttime navigation or security monitoring.

Adafruit VCNL4200 IR Proximity

Role: Long distance IR proximity and ambient light sensor.
Importance: Acts as the collision-avoidance radar. It accurately measures the distance to objects in front of the cart and adjusts to ambient light, preventing the cart from crashing into walls or obstacles.

Pi Stemma QT Breakout

Role: I2C interface board for the Raspberry Pi.
Importance: Simplifies hardware prototyping. It allows I2C sensors (like the VCNL4200) to be plugged directly into the Pi without messy soldering, ensuring reliable and secure data connections.

STEMMA QT Cables (150mm & 400mm)

Role: JST SH 4-pin data and power cables.
Importance: These cables route the communication lines from the Pi's brain to the sensors mounted on the edges of the cart. The different lengths (150mm and 400mm) provide flexibility in where sensors can be physically placed on the chassis.

Servo Hat

Role: PWM/Servo controller add-on board.
Importance: The Raspberry Pi struggles to control multiple servos precisely on its own. This HAT offloads that work, allowing smooth and accurate control of motors used for steering, moving the camera gimbal, or operating robotic arms on the cart.

Wagon Cart

Role: The physical chassis and base.
Importance: Provides the structural foundation and payload capacity. It houses the battery, the Pi, all the circuitry, and the screen, making the entire node completely mobile and robust for real-world testing.

10-Inch Touchscreen

Role: On-board Human-Machine Interface (HMI).
Importance: Allows direct interaction with the AxelNode operating system without needing a remote PC. Essential for on-the-fly debugging, starting scripts, and viewing camera feeds directly from the cart in the field.

5V 5A Dual PD Power Supply

Role: Step-down power converter (9-24V to 5V) with cooling.
Importance: The Pi 5, screen, and sensors draw significant power. This module safely steps down a larger battery voltage to a stable 5V at a high current (5A). The cooling fan ensures the power delivery remains stable under heavy compute loads, preventing system crashes.

Also utilizing a 6ft USB Extension Cable to neatly route power and peripheral connections across the length of the wagon cart.
